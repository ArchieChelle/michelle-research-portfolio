---
title: "Evaluating Tier-Feature Fit to Inform SE Play Pricing and Retention Strategy"
description: A feature usefulness survey found SE Play's premium tiers charging for the features users valued least — redirecting strategy from repricing to repackaging.
slug: seplay-pricing
heroLabel: Case Study
heroOutcome: The premium tiers' exclusive features were the ones users valued least. The findings pointed to repackaging over repricing, and stakeholders funded two follow-up studies.
timeline: ~3 months
client: NBCUniversal — SportsEngine Play
methods:
  - Evaluative Research
  - Feature Usefulness Survey
  - Tier-Fit Analysis
  - Survey Design
role: UX Researcher
sample: n=75 SE Play subscribers via in-product Qualtrics intercept (Relatives 66 · Fans 6 · Athletes 3); analysis focused on Relatives (88%), below the threshold for statistical significance
team: Sales Director, Price Valuation Team, Product, Research
challenge:
  - Team needed to map valued features to the right subscription tiers without overstating pricing evidence.
  - Signals like usefulness, willingness to pay, and churn were related but not equivalent.
approach:
  - Ran a feature usefulness survey rating 11 features across the Free, Premier, and All-Access tiers.
  - Sequenced follow-up research from broader recruitment and behavioral triangulation to a price-based conjoint for pricing validation.
findings:
  - The Free tier already fit — current free features were well-perceived (useful to ~80% of respondents).
  - The premium tiers under-delivered; among Premier features, Ad-Free Streaming was the only one rated as useful as not-useful (25% / 25%).
  - All-Access's exclusive features (training videos, downloads, on-demand library, premium events) ranked lowest — none cracked the top three, an inverted fit.
  - Upsell only worked one way — Free to Premier was plausible, Premier to All-Access was not — and Training Videos from World Class Athletes was the single least-useful feature.
dataAnalysis:
  - "Feature Value Score = share rating each feature 'Useful' (top-box) vs 'Not Useful,' per feature per tier."
  - "Evidence boundary: single-item ratings on a small, relative-heavy sample (n=75); directional and below statistical significance. The study did not measure willingness to pay or prove churn causality."
impact:
  - Recommended repackaging Premier and All-Access, where perceived value was weakest, rather than starting from price.
  - "Stakeholders approved two follow-up studies: a churn survey to learn why subscribers cancel, and a price-based conjoint survey to test willingness to pay."
